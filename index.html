Mission Statement

The first time I volunteered for the science class at the Friends of Children of Special Needs organization, I noticed a key problem. The students with special needs were learning about cohesion and had been given a definition about water molecules binding with other surfaces. This explanation was abstract and confusing. I intervened by reframing the idea to something familiar-- water sticking to a window after rain. After my explanation, I saw the concept click as students connected the scientific idea to an everyday experience. This moment reshaped how I think about teaching. I realized that learning barriers are often not caused by disability, but how knowledge is communicated. The students were capable of understanding complex ideas; they simply needed concepts presented in ways that matched how they processed information. Watching their understanding develop showed me how essential accessibility is in education. This experience sparked my interest in neuroscience, particularly in understanding how differences in cognition influence learning and accessibility. I hope to continue working with individuals with special needs through computational modeling, data science, neuroscience, and cognitive science, specifically in ways that can improve their quality of life.
